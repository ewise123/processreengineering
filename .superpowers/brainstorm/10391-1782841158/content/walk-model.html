"Walk the change" — how committal should the preview be?

A suggestion card proposes: "Add an Approval step between Submit and Process." Before the user clicks Apply, how do we show what it will do on the canvas? Pick the interaction model that feels right — we can refine the look after.

A

Highlight only — point at what's affected

Hovering / clicking the card pans to and glows the existing objects the change touches. No preview of new content; you read the card text for "what," the canvas tells you "where." Cheapest — reuses the navigate + flash we already have.

IntakeSubmit→Process→Notify
2 steps highlighted — glow only; nothing drawn yet
B

Ghost preview — draw the result, don't commit it

Clicking Preview renders the would-be outcome on the canvas as dashed "ghosts": the new Approval node and its edges appear; the edge being replaced fades/strikes through. Nothing is saved. Apply commits the ghosts; leaving the card reverts them. Shows the full shape of the change.

IntakeSubmit⇢Approve⇢Process→Notify
ApplyCancel previewghosts = not yet saved
C

Step-by-step walkthrough — one change at a time

For a multi-change bundle, a stepper walks through each op in order: "Step 2 of 3 — connect Submit → Approve." Each step ghosts just that one change and pans to it; Next advances. Best for understanding a big bundle, but heavier to build and slower to skim.

My lean: B (ghost preview) — it shows the actual shape of the change and reuses the aiProposed ghost-rendering the canvas already has, without the cost of a full stepper. But tell me how you picture it.
